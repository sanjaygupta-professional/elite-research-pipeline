# Success Criteria

*Defined before planning or implementation. The system is not "good" unless it passes these.*

---

## Component 1: Baseline Research

**What "good" looks like:**

| Criterion | What it means | How to validate independently |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------------------|
| **Coverage** | All 10 categories have a documented baseline state | Checklist — each category file exists and is non-empty |
| **Depth** | Each category summary enables a credible 15-minute conversation with a knowledgeable peer | Stress test: could you brief a domain expert without embarrassing gaps? |
| **Recency** | Captures the state of each category as of April 2026, not 2023 | Spot check: are the most significant developments of the past 12 months present in each file? |
| **Perspective balance** | Each category includes at least one optimist view, one skeptic view, one practitioner voice | Review: is there genuine intellectual diversity, or a single school of thought? |
| **POV readiness** | Reading any category baseline generates genuine hypotheses — things you'd argue for or against | Test: after reading a category, can you write 2–3 "I believe..." statements from memory without returning to the file? |

**Failure signals:**
- Generic summaries that could have been written by anyone with a search engine
- No original tension or debate represented
- Feels like a Wikipedia article — factual but opinionless
- Significant developments from 2025–2026 are missing

## Component 2: Source Set

**What "good" looks like:**

| Criterion | What it means | How to validate independently |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------------------|
| **Signal density** | Each source produces strong or weak signals at least 60% of the time | After 2 weeks of tracking: what % of items from each source are signal vs. noise? Sources below 60% are candidates for removal |
| **Coverage completeness** | Together, the sources cover all 10 categories | Matrix check: map each source to the categories it covers — identify gaps |
| **Perspective diversity** | Technology, business/org, academic, practitioner, contrarian voices — all represented | Categorize each source by perspective type — no single type should dominate (>50%) |
| **Time sustainability** | Total consumption fits within the available weekly time budget | Honest estimate: can you actually process all sources in the time you have, every week, sustainably? |
| **Uniqueness** | Each source adds something no other source covers | Removal test: if you removed this source, would you lose a perspective not covered elsewhere? |
| **Credibility floor** | No source survives purely on audience size — must have demonstrated expertise or primary insight | Can you name the genuine expertise or unique access behind this source? |

**Failure signals:**
- All sources agree with each other (echo chamber — they're all citing the same underlying sources)
- Any source produces >40% noise over a 2-week trial
- Sources you subscribe to but haven't opened in 3 weeks
- No academic or research-level source in the set
- No contrarian or skeptical voice in the set

## Component 3: Periodic Update Rhythm

**What "good" looks like:**

| Criterion | What it means | How to validate independently |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------------------|
| **Consistency** | The cadence is maintained for 4+ consecutive weeks without requiring heroic effort | Track dates: did each scheduled update cycle actually happen? |
| **Processing rate** | At least 50% of consumed items are converted into a structured note, intel card, or tagged signal — not just read and forgotten | Count: items consumed vs. items with a written output per week |
| **Signal accumulation** | Each week adds at least 3 new strong signals and 5 new weak signals to the system | Count: net new signals entering the system each cycle |
| **POV evolution** | Your articulated position on at least one topic shifts or deepens per month | Monthly review: compare your stated position on a topic now vs. 4 weeks ago — did it move? Is the movement evidence-based? |
| **Time efficiency** | The update cycle takes no more than 45–60 minutes per day for daily touchpoints, no more than 2–3 hours for weekly synthesis | Time-box and verify: does it actually fit an executive schedule? |

**Failure signals:**
- Lots of bookmarking, very little processing ("I'll read this later" with no later)
- Reading without forming any position — consumption without synthesis
- Cadence only holds when motivated, not by system design
- Weekly synthesis always getting skipped in favor of "real work"

## Component 4: Overall Knowledge System

**What "good" looks like:**

| Criterion | What it means | How to validate independently |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------------------|
| **Output quality** | One substantive thought leadership piece per week — grounded in specific signals, with Sanjay's own POV | Review 4 weeks of output: does each piece cite specific signals or experiments? Or is it opinion without evidence? |
| **Distinctiveness** | The writing is distinguishable from generic AI commentary | Test: give a post to someone who knows you — does it sound like Sanjay specifically, or like anyone with a ChatGPT subscription? |
| **Stakeholder resonance** | Internal and external audiences engage meaningfully — not just react | Track: are people responding with "I hadn't thought of that" vs. just liking? Are colleagues forwarding internally? |
| **Self-sufficiency** | The system runs without heroic effort — it's sustainable by design | After 8 weeks: is it still running, or has it collapsed into inconsistency when life gets busy? |
| **Compounding effect** | Each month, the baseline deepens — new knowledge connects to prior knowledge | Check: is new knowledge being linked to existing understanding, or treated as isolated new facts each time? |

**Failure signals:**
- Posts that could have been written by anyone with a ChatGPT subscription
- System only functions when there's extra time
- No visible evolution in the depth or originality of POV over 3 months
- Stakeholder feedback is polite but not substantive

## The Validation Contract

```
Step 1: DEFINE criteria         ← done (this document)
Step 2: CONFIRM criteria        ← Sanjay reviews and adjusts
Step 3: PLAN the system         ← source selection, cadence, structure
Step 4: IMPLEMENT               ← baseline research + tooling configuration
Step 5: VALIDATE against (1)    ← independent check, each criterion, documented result
        → Pass: proceed to next phase
        → Fail: diagnose which specific criterion failed and why, then fix
```

**Key principle:** The criteria in this document were defined before the work was done. They cannot be retroactively adjusted to make the work "pass." If the system fails a criterion, the answer is to fix the system — not rewrite the criterion.
