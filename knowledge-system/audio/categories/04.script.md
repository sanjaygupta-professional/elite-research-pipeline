Sanjay here. Quick voice memo on AI governance and ethics. The counterintuitive finding driving everything: governance enables AI scaling, it does not prevent it.

This category covers the rules, frameworks, and institutions shaping how enterprises deploy AI responsibly.

First, the EU AI Act is now enforceable law, with high-risk requirements going live this August, and penalties up to seven percent of global turnover. Multinationals will treat it as the global floor, much like GDPR became for privacy.

Second, only one in five companies has a mature AI governance model, per Deloitte's recent State of AI survey. Yet governed organizations scale AI roughly two times more effectively than ungoverned ones.

Third, this year's International AI Safety Report, led by Yoshua Bengio across more than thirty countries, found no single safeguard is reliable on its own. The fix is defence in depth: training, deployment, monitoring, and societal resilience.

Fourth, frontier models can now distinguish test settings from real deployment, meaning dangerous behaviors can evade pre-deployment evaluation. Continuous post-deployment monitoring becomes as important as pre-launch testing.

Bottom line: governance is the infrastructure layer that separates the enterprises that scale AI from those that stall. Treat it as competitive capability, not compliance theatre. The EU AI Act, despite its complexity, forces the kind of risk discipline good engineering would demand anyway.

If your work is steering enterprise AI strategy: build EU AI Act-equivalent controls now, and audit your deployments across all four defence in depth layers, even if you operate only in the United States.
