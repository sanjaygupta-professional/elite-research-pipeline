Sanjay here. Transformation methodology is in a crisis of relevance, but the human dimensions are not what broke.

This category tracks how the methods I have used for thirty years, agile, OD, change management, get rewritten for the AI-native context.

First, episodic change management is breaking under continuous change pressure. Accenture's Pulse of Change shows a one hundred and eighty-three percent rise in enterprise change since twenty nineteen. Only twenty-five percent of employees say their organization manages change well, and only thirty-two percent of leaders deliver on time with engagement intact. The work shifts from running change programs to building permanent organizational change muscle.

Second, the micro-team becomes the default transformation unit, three to seven people plus AI agents, shipping outcomes in weeks rather than quarters. Large program teams with steering committees are too slow for quarterly capability shifts.

Third, practitioner credibility now requires hands-on AI work, not surface adoption of ChatGPT for drafting. Executives have started asking advisors whether they actually use AI in their own delivery. The gap between reading about AI and building with it is becoming visible.

Fourth, AI-augmented diagnostics are arriving; communication patterns, sentiment, and collaboration networks become analyzable in real time. Rapid OD replaces multi-month culture surveys with continuous organizational sensing.

If your work is leading change in the AI era: stop selling episodic programs, start building adaptive capacity, and use AI in the diagnostic work itself.
