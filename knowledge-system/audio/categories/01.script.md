Sanjay here. The technology is now significantly more capable than most organizations have adapted to use.

Generative AI sits at the center of executive strategy and the daily work of knowledge teams. First, reasoning models are a real inflection, not just better autocomplete. OpenAI's o3 hit 87.5% on the ARC-AGI-1 benchmark, near the human average of 84%, on tasks designed to resist memorization. Second, DeepSeek R1 cracked open the cost story in January. An open-weight reasoning model, reportedly trained for around six million dollars, runs on a consumer GPU. That shifts who can deploy serious AI without an API tether. Third, context windows reached practical scale. Gemini 2.5 Pro now handles one million tokens in a single pass, so workflows that needed complex retrieval architectures fit in plain context. Fourth, agents are real but bounded. The best coding agents pass roughly 55% on SWE-bench Verified, useful with oversight, not yet set and forget. Fifth, the productivity evidence is hardening. Replicated studies show twenty-five to forty percent task speedups for knowledge workers using these tools.

The skeptics, LeCun and Marcus, are right that these systems do not reason causally. For most knowledge work, that matters less than reliability on scoped tasks, and reliability is rising. The deeper constraint is organizational, not technical. Capability is ahead of process design and change readiness by a wide margin.
