Sanjay here. The big finding this year is simple: the difference was never the AI model, it was always the organization.

This category sits at the seam where enterprise AI meets organization design.

First, eighty-eight percent of companies use AI somewhere, yet eighty percent of projects fail to deliver value. Second, Stanford studied fifty-one enterprise deployments and concluded that organizational readiness, not model choice, decided who won. Third, agentic AI has become an operating model question; thirty-eight percent of companies are piloting, but only eleven percent have agents in production. Fourth, micro-teams of three to seven people, supported by AI agents, are shipping production work in weeks instead of quarters. Fifth, fifty-four percent of the C-suite now say AI adoption is tearing their company apart, and that tension may actually be a signal of real transformation.

The pattern across every major report is the same. Process redesign beats process layering. Governance enables scale; it does not slow it down. Moderna merged HR and IT under one leader, treating agents as a new class of digital worker. Most enterprises sit in the cosmetic middle, spending without restructuring, and capturing nothing.

If your work is enterprise transformation: stop deploying AI onto legacy processes, and start redesigning one function around a micro-team and agents. If your work is people strategy: build the joint HR and IT muscle now, before the C-suite forces it.
