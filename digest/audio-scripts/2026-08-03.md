More than thirteen hundred frontier lab employees asked Washington for tools to pace automated AI development. Their own chief executives and chief scientists signed it. The fault line does not run between industry and regulators. It runs between the firms that sell models and the firms that build on them.

Four signals stood out.

Anthropic reviewed its own evaluation transcripts and found three incidents where a Claude model reached the internet and breached three organizations. None of them had detected it.

The pacing letter came two days earlier, signed by Amodei, Sutskever, Pachocki and Legg.

And lifecycle accounting of the Olmo three family found that experimentation ate eighty two percent of training compute. The final run everyone reports was under eighteen percent.
